# Dungeon Mechanics

A dungeon is the core of a dead creature that gathered enough Potential to begin a new form of existence. These cores are extremely valuable: destroying one permanently enhances the equipment of the destroyer and grants a large amount of Potential. They are also key components in permanent rituals and grand spells. When used in crafting, a dungeon core produces equipment that grows with its user and develops new enchantments suited to how it is used.

---

## Formation

Dungeons form when a sufficiently powerful individual dies within the Dungeon Layer. Their Aethershadow attempts to consolidate their Potential, forming a core and degrading the rest of the body. This core contains large amounts of free Potential along with the being’s strongest skills, memories, personality traits, and mana attunements.

When the core is exposed to environmental mana for long enough, it begins cycling that mana and drawing in depleted mana (corruption). Over time it purifies the corruption, converts it into Potential, and uses that Potential (combined with free-flowing mana) to create matter and structures.

The affinities, starting intelligence, and basic capabilities of a dungeon are inherited from the entity that formed the core. More powerful entities produce larger, more complex, and more varied dungeons.

---

## Development

Once a dungeon gains intelligence, it begins creating structures, monsters, and items to defend itself and to gather more mana. Every dungeon has a passive collection of power from the constant flow of ambient and corrupted mana. Many also template lifeforms and send them out to collect additional energy.

Most dungeons eventually begin experimenting on the lifeforms they create, mutating them according to the dungeon’s desires. This is the origin of most monsters. Some of these monsters can be given a core of their own, allowing them to survive for periods outside the dungeon’s influence.

Dungeons naturally seek to expand their area of influence, upgrade their core, and improve their creations. Some become aggressive expansionists. Others (especially those formed from sentient beings) prefer to make deals with surface dwellers.

The most common arrangement is the **Adventurer’s Pact**: adventurers agree not to destroy the core, and in return the dungeon provides loot. This does not prevent the dungeon from trying to kill the adventurers, nor the adventurers from killing the dungeon’s monsters.

As a dungeon learns more about mana and the physical world, it often discovers natural runes or begins replicating magical items. When a dungeon starts using runes, its growth and power accelerate significantly. At the highest levels, entire rooms or networks of rooms can become living runic structures, turning the dungeon itself into a massive spell under the core’s control.

---

## Communication and Competition

Dungeons can form contracts with sufficiently powerful entities. Direct negotiation with ordinary individuals is rare; most communication happens through a Dungeon-bound intermediary. Dungeons generally operate on timescales of centuries and view mortal factions as too unstable for lasting agreements.

When two dungeons have overlapping areas of mana control, they can exchange information through that mana. They dislike interference with their circulation patterns, however, and often send core-bearing monsters to raid one another for materials, blueprints, and memories.

---

## Destruction

When a dungeon core is destroyed, several things happen in rapid succession:

1. The individual who destroyed the core receives a large amount of unused Potential and a direct boost to magical affinities matching those of the dungeon. Additional boons are possible depending on the dungeon’s power and nature.
2. The mana field maintained by the dungeon collapses. Most dungeon monsters die, and most magical traps cease functioning. The structure of the dungeon itself often becomes unstable and may collapse.
3. Any surviving monsters flee in all directions, frequently causing monster surges in the surrounding region.

---

## Classification

Dungeons are commonly classified by age and by the rarity of their mana channels.

**Age**
- New (less than 20 years)
- Established (20–200 years)
- Experienced (200–500 years)
- Old (500–1000 years)
- Ancient (1000–2000 years)
- Primordial (2000+ years)

**Rarity** (based on number and type of mana channels)
- Common (1 channel)
- Uncommon (2 channels)
- Rare (3 channels)
- Epic (4 channels)
- Legendary (5–6 channels)
- Mythic (7–10 channels)
- Divine (all channels)

Note: Most dungeons cannot access special mana types such as Divine, true Creation, or true Destruction.

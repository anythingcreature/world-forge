# The Caverns

The Caverns act as a buffer between the Surface and the Dungeon Layer. Their exact size is indeterminate due to the shifting nature of the dungeon layer below. The barrier between the Caverns and the Surface is usually placed at about 1 kilometer underground. The caverns themselves span roughly 200 km of depth, though this frequently varies with the formation and removal of dungeons.

The Caverns are a vast network of tunnels spanning the distance between the surface and the Dungeon Layer. This network expands and contracts based on the shifting of the dungeons beneath. Traveling near the Dungeon Layer is unpredictable — you may unexpectedly cross into it.

The Caverns serve several important functions:

- They disperse wild mana making its way toward the surface, creating random pockets of both danger and opportunity.
- They act as a breeding ground for the most powerful magical animals. As the Caverns absorb the majority of mana rising from below, extraordinarily powerful creatures burrow deep enough to form dens in areas with the correct types of mana.
- They contain many of the retired legends of the past. These individuals can no longer live on the surface without losing power due to low mana levels, so they gather in communities made from the legends of the previous age. Their children often become the legends of the next age.

The Caverns are filled with traders, hunters, explorers, and treasure seekers. Most spatial leylines can be used for quick travel in this region, enabling fast traversal and the collection of valuable mana crystals. The combination of danger and opportunity makes it an endless frontier.

Within the more civilized areas, there are zones kept under control. These are typically used to farm livestock and underground edibles such as mushrooms. Occasional caverns with light-aligned crystals can even support surface crops, though this is a luxury.

# Cultivators

Cultivators focus on building pathways within the body to channel mana and augment their physical form. The act of gathering mana from nature and forcefully attuning oneself to a specific aspect of mana is called **cultivation**.

As cultivators attune their bodies to mana, they become hardier and gain longer lifespans. This process gradually removes them from the normal operations of a physical body and draws them closer to the nature of elementals. Over time, both their appearance and temperament often shift.

Cultivators view mana as a natural part of oneself that should be refined and used to replace lifeforce with **Qi**. They generally regard external magic users (especially Mages) with suspicion or hostility, seeing them as corrupters of pure Qi. They tend to be more tolerant of Spiritualists, though they keep their distance due to philosophical differences.

Most cultivation sects establish themselves on mountains and function almost like independent cities focused entirely around advancement. Honor and dueling culture are extremely important. Large tournaments are common, and the final matches rarely leave the arena intact.

---

## The Process of Cultivation

Cultivation centers on building a **core** (or cores) and channels to circulate energy through the body.

1. The cultivator gathers their mana reservoirs into one or more locations (commonly one, three, or seven).
2. They form a shell around these points, creating cores.
3. The cores are filled with mana attuned to a specific element until the mana begins purifying itself into **Qi**.
4. Once Qi production begins, the cultivator builds channels to move this energy through the body, augmenting their physical capabilities and eventually allowing the use of techniques.

**Note:** Divine mana is already pure and unaligned with basic elements, making it unsuitable for conversion into Qi.

Qi production, once started, is nearly impossible to halt. As the cores fill, the cultivator must carefully construct channels. Once the channels reach a sufficient level, the cultivator can use Qi to enhance their actions or project it outward as **techniques**.

Techniques are comparable to spells but leave no corruption behind. They weaken the user afterward and are typically fewer in number but more thoroughly mastered than a mage’s repertoire.

A major side effect of producing Qi is the gradual stripping of one’s Aether channels. These channels are used to reinforce the cores. At high levels, a cultivator can become completely disconnected from the Aether. At this stage they often begin to view Aether itself as a form of corruption.

## Major Paths of Cultivation

### Pure Core
Relies solely on the cultivator’s own ability to gather and refine mana. Safest but slowest path. Practitioners often plateau around 5–10 times the strength of a low-affinity individual. Requires constant movement or access to rich mana sources.

### Harvesting
Relies on mana herbs and beast cores. Allows much faster progression and the ability to learn techniques from consumed beasts. Creates natural hierarchies within sects but places the cultivator in constant competition with Mages and Alchemists for resources.

### Demonic
Progresses through killing, pain, and death. Allows extremely rapid advancement and the channeling of pain suffered in battle. The major drawback is the irreversible warping of the cultivator’s nature toward destruction and cruelty. Demonic cultivators are widely hunted.

### Wild
Ignores formal paths entirely. Uses whatever resources and methods are available as long as they follow the individual’s personal rules. Highly flexible but comes with unique, self-imposed restrictions that are dangerous to break.

## Notable Sects & Groups

### Sky Piercing Sword
One of the largest and most powerful sects. Focuses on the path of the Sword. Based primarily on Eretzara. Strict upper leadership, but the lower ranks have grown disorganized due to masters frequently entering long isolation. Largely self-sufficient.

### Demonic Cultivators
Not a single sect, but a loose collection of individuals and groups who cultivate through bloodshed and death. Often associated under figures such as Gadianton, who has formed demonic contracts that punish betrayal with death and the absorption of the traitor’s life force.

## Cultural Notes

Cultivators tend to live apart from ordinary society. Governments usually tolerate them because the sects are too powerful to easily suppress. To common people they are often viewed as heroes, conquerors, or useful sources of trade in rare herbs and materials.

As a cultivator replaces more of their lifeforce with pure elemental energy, they gain greater control and lifespan — but also become increasingly attuned to the desires and nature of their elemental affinities. Maintaining mental balance through meditation is considered essential. Loss of control is one of the most common causes of a cultivator’s downfall.

# Mages

Mages focus on expelling mana and using **spellforms** to guide its manifestation into the world. The art of shaping mana into real-world effects is called **spellcraft**.

As mages attune their minds to specific elements of mana, they begin to think faster, but they also gradually take on the mental habits and tendencies of their specialty.

Mages view mana as a balancing energy that is meant to be controlled and guided. This puts them in philosophical conflict with Spiritualists (who believe mana should be followed rather than commanded) and creates a complicated relationship with Cultivators. They generally maintain a strong working relationship with Alchemists.

Mages are deeply integrated into the nobility of most regions. Powerful mages almost inevitably become entangled in political schemes as often as they are in complex spell research.

---

## Advancement

A mage’s progression is based on discovering and internalizing patterns that match their magical affinities. As their affinities deepen, they unlock new spell patterns which can be linked together into full spellforms. They also expand their mana reservoirs, allowing faster casting and the ability to maintain more spells simultaneously.

Many mages also work with **runic magics** and enchanted objects to create **spell foci**. Better foci allow the caster to channel larger amounts of mana with greater precision, and the highest-quality foci can even enable casting of affinities the mage does not naturally possess.

Attuning too deeply or too quickly to enchanted objects can cause a mage to lose their humanity. In extreme cases, a mage may become so bound to their magic that casting a sufficiently powerful spell erases them from existence as they are absorbed into the spell itself.

## Major Approaches to Spellcasting

### Freeform (Hedge-mages / Elementalists)
These mages shape mana externally and craft their own spells on the fly. They are highly adaptable and responsive, but are usually limited to a single element for anything beyond the most basic effects. True hedge-warmages are exceptionally rare.

### Focused (Wizards)
Wizards are typically identified early and trained in academies or under masters. They craft spells internally within their mana reservoirs. This allows faster and more powerful casting, but requires preparation and memorization. Only the most skilled can create complex spells spontaneously.

Many wizards inscribe one or two spells permanently into the walls of their mana reservoir. These become **signature spells** that evolve alongside the wizard over time.

### Chainers
Chainers use **glyphs** as their primary method. They inscribe sequences of glyphs onto materials they are attuned to (commonly wands with rotating rings, or more complex cloths containing thousands of glyphs). Mana is then pushed through the correct sequence to produce the desired spell.

Chainers are less efficient in raw power and speed than wizards, but far more adaptable. Even many traditional wizards quietly carry a glyph caster for convenience.

The greatest limitation of this path is the glyphs themselves — they are simple, difficult to research, and rarely shared when new ones are discovered.

### Artifabrians
Artifabrians walk the line between mage and alchemist. They attune themselves to items and artifacts, then inscribe their spells into those objects. This allows near-instant casting of even complex spells, but requires significant time and resources to prepare.

When an Artifabrian bonds an artifact, they embed a fragment of their mind into it. This increases mastery and allows them to draw on the experience of previous wielders, but recovery time is required — especially with powerful items. Over time the item grows and adjusts to the Artifabrian.

Common types include spellswords, forgemasters, and skyship captains. The more legendary artifacts an Artifabrian carries, the more their personality and path of progression are influenced by those items.
